Privacy Policy

Effective 27 October 2026

qMail is an email app for macOS, made by Honza Kubita (“we”). This policy explains what data qMail accesses, how it is used, where it is stored and who it is shared with. It covers the qMail app and this website.

In short: qMail runs entirely on your Mac. It connects directly to your email provider and to no server of ours. We never receive, see, store, sell or share your email, contacts or account details.

Data qMail accesses

When you add an email account, qMail accesses:

  • Your email: messages and their headers (sender, recipients, subject, date), message text and attachments, read/starred status and the folders or labels they're in.
  • Your account details: your email address and name, and the server settings of your email provider.
  • Your sign-in: either OAuth tokens (when you use Sign in with Google) or the password or app password you enter for other providers.

Google user data

If you add a Google account with Sign in with Google, qMail asks Google for these permissions:

  • https://mail.google.com/ (read, compose, send and permanently delete all your email from Gmail), so qMail can use Gmail's IMAP and SMTP servers;
  • openid, email and profile, to learn your email address and name.

How qMail uses Google user data. Only to provide the features you see in the app: downloading and showing your email as chats, threads and folders; searching it; and, when you ask, sending, replying to, forwarding, marking, moving, archiving or deleting messages. Your name and email address are used to identify the account and as the sender of email you write.

How qMail stores Google user data. Email headers, the messages you open, and a search index of your email's text are stored on your Mac, inside qMail's sandboxed app container. (To make search cover your whole mailbox, qMail downloads the text of every email for the index; you can turn this off in qMail › Settings › General.) OAuth tokens are stored in the macOS Keychain. Nothing is stored anywhere else.

How qMail shares Google user data. It doesn't. Your email travels only between your Mac and Google's servers, over encrypted connections. It is never sent to us or to any third party, and qMail contains no analytics, advertising or tracking code.

What we don't do. We don't sell Google user data, use it for advertising, use it to determine credit-worthiness or for lending, or use it to develop, improve or train artificial intelligence or machine learning models. No person, including us, reads your email; we have no access to it.

qMail's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Other email providers

For iCloud, Fastmail, Yahoo and other IMAP accounts, qMail handles your email the same way: it's stored only on your Mac, your password is kept in the macOS Keychain, and your email is exchanged only with your provider's servers.

Other connections qMail makes

Besides your email provider, qMail connects to:

  • GitHub, once a day, to check for a new version of qMail (api.github.com), and to download an update if you install one. GitHub sees your IP address and qMail's version. You can turn automatic checks off in qMail › Settings › General. See GitHub's privacy statement.
  • Mozilla's ISP database (autoconfig.thunderbird.net), only when you add an account from a provider qMail doesn't know, to look up its server settings. It receives the domain part of your email address (for example example.com), not the full address.
  • Remote images in emails, only if you click to load them or turn on “Load remote images automatically”. The senders' servers then see that the email was opened, as with any email app.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • qMail keeps the data described above for as long as the account is added in qMail.
  • Removing an account in qMail › Settings › Accounts deletes everything qMail stored for it: cached email, the search index, and its tokens or password in the Keychain.
  • To withdraw qMail's access to your Google account, open myaccount.google.com/permissions and remove qMail. qMail then can't access your Gmail until you sign in again.
  • Deleting the qMail app and its folder ~/Library/Containers/com.honzakubita.qmail removes all of its data from your Mac.

Because we never receive your data, there is nothing for us to delete on our side.

Security

qMail connects to Google and the other built-in providers only over TLS-encrypted connections. (For a custom server you set up by hand, you choose the connection security, which can include an unencrypted connection if that server requires one.) It keeps passwords and tokens in the macOS Keychain, and runs in the macOS App Sandbox, which keeps other apps from reading its data. HTML emails are shown with JavaScript turned off.

This website

This website is a static site hosted by GitHub Pages. It has no cookies, analytics or forms. GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses as described in its privacy statement.

Children

qMail isn't directed at children under 13 and doesn't knowingly collect any data from them; it doesn't collect data from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published on this page with a new effective date. Changes to how qMail uses Google user data will be made here before the app changes.
